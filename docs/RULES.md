# Palworld OFFICIAL CARD GAME — Rules Summary

A plain-English guide to the full rules, based on the official **Comprehensive Rules ver. 1.00 (July 30, 2026)**. Section numbers like (§9.4) point back to that document if you ever need the exact wording.

> **Golden rule:** if a card's text disagrees with these rules, the card wins (§1.3.1). If a card says you *can't* do something and another says you *must*, "can't" wins (§1.3.3).

---

## 1. How to win

It's a 1-vs-1 game. You win when your opponent loses, and a player loses when **either**:

- their **life drops to 0 or less** (everyone starts at **10 life**), or
- their **deck runs out of cards** (§1.2.1).

If both lose at the same moment, it's a draw. You can concede at any time.

The twist: **damage to a player doesn't come off their life directly.** It makes them flip cards off their deck first, and lucky cards can cancel it. See [§10 Damage & life](#10-damage-to-players-mill-and-the-lucky-pal-save). Because damage burns through your deck, decking out is a real way to lose.

## 2. What you need

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| **Main deck** | Exactly **50 cards** |
| **Soul deck** | **10 Soul cards**, your "mana" supply |
| Counters | Life (starts at 10), Material, Ingredient |

## 3. Card types and what's on a card

There are five card types (§2.2):

| Type | What it is | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| **Pal** | Your creatures. They attack, block, and work at structures. Either a **Normal Pal** or a **Lucky Pal**. | Base |
| **Structure** | Buildings. Pals can be *assigned* to them to activate effects. Can be attacked. | Base |
| **Gear** | Equipment and weapons. They sit in your base and usually *rest* to give a one-off boost or effect. | Base |
| **Event** | One-shot spells. Do the effect, then go to the graveyard. | Graveyard after use |
| **Soul** | Your resource cards. Not part of the main deck. | Soul deck → soul area |

### Card anatomy

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Cost** (◇ number) | How many **souls** you must rest to play it. The cost's background colour is the card's **colour**. |
| **Colour** | Red, Blue, Green or Purple (or colourless). Effects reference it, e.g. "all your blue Pals get +200 power". The rules have no colour restriction on decks. |
| **Power** | A Pal's fighting strength. It's both **the damage it deals** to Pals and structures **and how much damage it can take** before it dies. |
| **Durability** | A structure's version of power: how much damage it can take. |
| **Strike** | How much damage a Pal deals **to a player**. Usually 1–3. |
| **Element** | Neutral, Fire, Water, Grass, Electric, Ground, Ice, Dark, Dragon. "Neutral" is a real element, not "no element". |
| **Work Suitability** | Jobs the Pal is good at (Kindling, Electricity, Mining, Transporting…). Effects reference these. |
| **Lucky icon** | Marks a **Lucky Pal**. Matters when a player takes damage (§10). |
| **Quick icon** | The card or ability can be used during the opponent's attack (§9, Quick step). |

**Name rule:** a name like `Orserk – Thunderclap Roar` has a *main name* ("Orserk") and a *sub name* ("Thunderclap Roar"). Deck limits use the **full name**. Effects may say "main name 《Orserk》" to cover every Orserk.

## 4. Zones (the playmat)

Each player has their own:

| Zone | Public? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Deck** | Hidden | Your 50-card main deck. Order matters and can't be looked at unless an effect says so. |
| **Hand** | Hidden from opponent | No hand size limit is defined. |
| **Base** | Public | Your Pals, structures and gears. Cards here are **standing** (upright, ready) or **rested** (sideways, used). |
| **Soul deck** | Public | Your remaining Soul cards. |
| **Soul area** | Public | Souls you've gained. Standing souls are available to spend; rested souls are spent for this turn. |
| **Graveyard** | Public | Used and defeated cards, face up. |
| **Exile** | Public | Cards removed from the game. |

**Pal limit:** you can have at most **5 Pals** in your base (§4.4.5). If you go over, you choose which 5 stay and the rest go to the graveyard. Structures and gears don't count toward this limit.

A card that leaves the base becomes a "new card": all damage, buffs and effects on it are gone (§4.1.4).

---

## 5. Building a deck (§6.1)

- **Exactly 50 cards** in the main deck.
- **Max 4 copies** of any card with the same name.
- **Max 8 Lucky Pals** total.
- Plus a separate **soul deck of 10 Soul cards**.

---

## 6. Setting up a game (§6.2)

1. Shuffle your main deck.
2. Randomly pick a player; **they choose** who goes first.
3. Put your 10 souls in your soul deck.
4. **The player going second puts 1 soul into their soul area** to make up for going second.
5. Both players **draw 5**.
6. **Mulligan:** starting with the first player, each player may *once* shuffle their whole hand back and draw a fresh 5.
7. Life = **10**, damage taken = 0, Material = 0, Ingredient = 0.
8. The first player starts.

---

## 7. Turn structure (§7)

Each turn has five phases:

### ① Stand phase
**Stand (untap) everything**: all your base cards and all your souls. "At the start of turn" effects trigger.

### ② Draw phase
**Draw 1 card.** The player going first **skips this on their very first turn**.

### ③ Soul phase
**Move 2 souls** from your soul deck into your soul area. Once the soul deck runs out, you stop gaining souls; you only ever have 10.

### ④ Main phase
Do any of the following, **as many times as you like, in any order** (§8):

- **Play a card** from your hand: rest souls equal to its cost.
  - Pals, structures and gears enter your base, **standing**.
  - Events resolve and go to the graveyard.
- **Use an activated ability** (ACT) on one of your cards by paying its cost.
- **Start a battle** with a standing Pal (§9). **Pals can attack the turn they're played**; there's no summoning sickness.
- **Pay 3 souls to draw 1 card.** You can do this **once per turn**.

### ⑤ End phase
- "At the end of turn" effects trigger.
- **All damage on Pals and structures is wiped** (it resets every turn).
- "Until end of turn" effects expire.
- Play passes to your opponent.

## 8. Souls & resources

### Souls = your mana
- Card costs and many ability costs are paid in **souls**: you **rest** that many standing souls.
- A circled number like **①** or **②** in an ability cost means "rest that many souls".
- Souls stand again in your next stand phase.
- Growth: going second you start with 1, then gain +2 per turn until all 10 are out.

### Material & Ingredient
Two counters you can stockpile (§3.3). They **start at 0**, **don't reset between turns**, and are public.

- **Gained** through card effects. For example, *Coal Quarry*: "[Assign 1 Pal] Get 3 Material, and draw 1 card"; *Flambelle*: "[Rest this card] Get 2 Material".
- **Spent ("consumed")** on powerful effects, e.g. *Mossanda Lux*: "[Consume 2 Material] deal 400 damage to up to 2 Pals".
- If you don't have enough, you can't pay.

### Structures and assigning Pals (§5.17)
Many structures have abilities with the cost **[Assign 1 Pal]**:

1. Choose one of your **standing** Pals.
2. **Rest it.** It's now working at that structure.
3. Get the effect.

So a rested worker Pal **can't attack or block** that turn. That's the core trade-off: **fight with your Pals, or put them to work for resources and cards.**

## 9. Battle, step by step (§9)

During your main phase you can declare a battle with one of your **standing** Pals.

### Step 1 — Attack declaration
- Choose your **attacker** (a standing Pal you control) and a **target**:
  - the **opponent** themselves, **or**
  - one of their **rested** Pals or structures.
- You **can't attack standing Pals** unless your attacker has **Assault**.
- **Rest your attacker.** "On Attack" effects trigger.

> Standing Pals are "on guard" and can't be targeted. Rested Pals (ones that attacked, worked at structures, etc.) are vulnerable.

### Step 2 — Block declaration
- The **defending player** may choose one of their **standing** Pals to **block**.
- The blocker **rests** and **becomes the new target**.
- A Pal with **Stealth** can't be blocked.

### Step 3 — Quick step
- **Only the defender** can act here. They may play **Quick** events or **Quick** abilities, as many as they like.
- Typical uses: tricks, pumps, or **Interrupt** to cancel the attack.
- The attacker can't respond.

### Step 4 — Damage
Depends on who's being attacked:

| Target | What happens |
|---|---|
| **A Pal** | Both Pals deal damage **equal to their power** to each other at the same time. |
| **A structure** | Attacker deals damage equal to its power. The structure **does not hit back**. |
| **The player** | Attacker deals damage **equal to its Strike** to the player (see §10). |

A Pal with 0 or less power deals no damage.

### Step 5 — End of battle
"End of battle" effects resolve. Then you're back in your main phase, free to do more things or start another battle.

### When does a Pal or structure die? (§11.4)
If a Pal or structure has **damage ≥ its power (or durability)**, and that damage is more than 0, it goes to the **graveyard**.

- Remember damage **clears at end of turn**, so a Pal that survives isn't permanently weakened.
- Example: a 600-power Pal blocks a 1000-power attacker. The blocker takes 1000 and dies; the attacker takes 600 and survives with 400 to spare.
- If a −power effect drops a Pal to 0 or below but it has **no damage**, it doesn't die from this rule.

## 10. Damage to players: mill and the Lucky Pal save

This is the most unusual rule in the game (§11.2). When a player takes damage (say, **3 damage** from a Strike 3 Pal):

1. Put the **top card** of their deck into the graveyard.
2. **Is it a Lucky Pal?**
   - **Yes:** the damage is **cancelled completely**. They **lose no life**. Stop.
   - **No:** keep going.
3. Repeat until they've milled cards **equal to the damage** (3 cards here), or their deck is empty.
4. If no Lucky Pal showed up, they **lose life equal to the damage**.

So every hit costs the defender deck cards **and** probably life. That's why decks are limited to **8 Lucky Pals**: they're your insurance.

- **Example:** you're hit for 3 and flip: Event, Structure, **Lucky Pal** → you lose 0 life, but 3 cards still went to the graveyard.
- **Example:** you're hit for 2 and flip: Pal, Gear → you lose 2 life.

Running out of deck cards is a loss (checked right after), so a long game can end by mill.

---

## 11. Automatic game rules ("rule actions", §11)

The game checks these constantly, and they happen without anyone choosing:

| Rule action | What happens |
|---|---|
| Player damage | Resolve the mill / Lucky Pal process (§10) immediately. |
| Lethal damage | Pals and structures with damage ≥ power/durability go to the graveyard. |
| Too many Pals | More than 5 Pals → owner chooses 5 to keep, the rest go to the graveyard. |
| Loss check | Life ≤ 0 or deck = 0 → that player loses. |

---

## 12. Abilities and timing (§10)

Card text uses three ability icons:

| Icon | Type | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| **ACT** | Activated | You choose to use it during your main phase by paying the cost in `[ ]`. "1 Turn" (once per turn) limits apply where printed. |
| **AUTO** | Automatic | Triggers by itself when something happens ("When…", "At the start of…", "On Deploy", "On Attack"). It's **mandatory** unless it says "you may" or has an optional cost. |
| **CONT** | Continuous | Always on while the card is in play, e.g. "all your blue Pals get +200 power". |

Other icons you'll see in text:
- **Hand** icon: the ability works **from your hand** (e.g. cost reductions).
- **Turn** icon (**1 Turn**): once per turn.
- **Quick**: usable in the Quick step.

**Ordering (§10.5):** whenever things happen, the game first applies rule actions. Then the **turn player** resolves their triggered AUTO abilities one at a time (in the order they choose), then the opponent resolves theirs. Then whoever has priority gets to act.

**"Choose" effects:** you must pick as many targets as you can, up to the number. "Choose up to X" lets you pick 0.

## 13. Keywords (§12)

| Keyword | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Quick** | — | Can be used during the defender's Quick step. |
| **On Deploy** | AUTO | Triggers when the card enters your base. |
| **On Attack** | AUTO | Triggers when this Pal attacks. |
| **On Assign** | AUTO | Triggers when this Pal is assigned to a structure. |
| **Brave X** | AUTO | When this Pal attacks, it gets **+X power** until end of turn. |
| **Serious X** | AUTO | When this Pal is assigned, choose 1 Pal: it gets **+X power** until end of turn. |
| **Interrupt** | ACT (Quick, from hand) | Discard this card and pay ① **or** discard it plus 1 other card: **cancel the opponent's attack**. No battle damage happens. |
| **Vigilance** | AUTO | At the end of your turn, **stand this card**, so it can block next turn even after attacking. |
| **Taunt** | CONT | If the opponent can attack this card, they **must** pick it as the target. |
| **Stealth** | CONT | This Pal **can't be blocked**. |
| **Retaliate** | AUTO | If this Pal dies in battle, the Pal it was fighting **also dies**. |
| **Nocturnal** | CONT | If it's **night**, this Pal gets **+300 power**. |
| **Breakthrough** | AUTO | If this Pal is attacking and destroys the opposing Pal, it **also deals its Strike to the opponent**. |
| **Assault** | CONT | Can attack **standing** Pals, not just rested ones. |

## 14. Night

"Night" is a game-wide state (§5.3) that affects **both players**. Cards turn it on, for example:

- *Lamp*: "[Mill 3] It becomes night until the end of the opponent's next turn."
- *Black Marketeer* (event) also brings night.

While it's night, **Nocturnal** Pals get +300 power, and many cards have bonus "if it is night" effects. Dark and purple decks lean on this heavily.

## 15. A worked example turn

You're going **second**, on your first turn. You start with **1 soul** from setup.

1. **Stand:** nothing to stand yet.
2. **Draw:** draw 1 (6 cards in hand).
3. **Soul:** +2 souls → **3 standing souls**.
4. **Main:**
   - Play *Coal Quarry* (cost 3): rest all 3 souls. It enters your base.
   - No souls left, so you can't play more cards, but abilities without soul costs still work.
   - Suppose you already had a Pal out: you could **attack** with it, or **assign** it to Coal Quarry for 3 Material + 1 card. Assigning rests it, so it can't attack this turn.
5. **End:** Coal Quarry's AUTO gives +1 Material. Damage clears. Opponent's turn.

**On their turn**, if your Pal is rested from working, it's an easy target for attacks. If it's standing, they can't attack it directly (barring Assault), and it's available to block.
